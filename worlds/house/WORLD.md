# THE GREAT INDOORS

> Page II of the atlas. A modern human house, explored at ant height, by
> anthropomorphic ants. It answers to [PHILOSOPHY.md](../../PHILOSOPHY.md)
> in everything, and to the [Text Box Law](../../DIALOGUE.md) in every
> line.

You came in with the shopping. Somewhere between the market and the
kitchen counter you were in a bag with a strawberry the size of a barn,
and then the bag opened, and the light was enormous, and now you live
here: **the Great Indoors** — which is what the locals call the world,
because as far as anyone can prove, the world is a house.

It is a modern house. It has a fridge that sings one note forever, a
robot that vacuums on a schedule, a router with opinions expressed
entirely in blinking, two Humans (weather), one Dog (weather with a
face), and — at ant height — more country than anyone could walk in a
lifetime. A countertop is a mesa. A couch is a mountain range with a
lost civilization under it. The gap behind the skirting board is a town.

Companion documents: [COLOR.md](COLOR.md) — the color script;
[VOICES.md](VOICES.md) — who talks and how; [LEDGER.md](LEDGER.md) —
the mysteries, audited.

## 1. The shape of the world

Two floors, an attic, and the rumor of Outside. The ants hold the walls,
the floors belong to everyone brave, and the vertical is free: **ants
climb** — walls, table legs, curtains, the bookshelf face — so the world
is explored in three dimensions and the ceiling is a road.

```
            THE ATTIC ─ dust-sheet ghosts · Rove's pages · THE DOLLHOUSE
               │ (the hatch; the warm vent)
   UPSTAIRS ───┤
   the STUDY ─ bookshelf city · the desk · the Blinkbox · the lamp
   the BATHROOM ─ steam jungle · the Backwards Town · the Deep drain
               │ (the stairs — switchbacks; the banister — the fast way)
   DOWNSTAIRS ─┤
   the KITCHEN ─ counter mesas · the Coldworks · the sugar mine · the sill garden
   the LIVING ROOM ─ carpet savanna · Couch Mountains · the Screen · the Bright
   the HALLWAY ─ the Big Door · the mail slot · the key bowl · Roomba dock
               │ (inside the wall)
   SKIRTING TOWN ─ the colony · matchbox hall · the fairy-light main street
               │
   OUTSIDE ─ visible through glass. Green. Loud. Later.
```

**Why a house:** a house is a valley that somebody vacuums. It has tides
(the Big Door, twice a day), weather (the Humans), geography (furniture),
seasons (laundry day, guest day, the terrible glory of Vacuum Day), and a
sea it can see but not touch (Outside, through the window — the House's
"Across"). Everything the philosophy needs, at a scale where a dropped
raisin is an event with a name.

**Scale and measure:** distances are measured in **legs** (a leg = a good
walk between rests — "the fridge is three legs from town"). Height is
measured in **books**. Nobody measures time; time is what the Household
does (see §6).

**No money.** The colony runs on **the Share** (crumbs carried to the
common store because that's what hands are for) and on favors, which is
to say: strings. The atlas's custom holds here too — a promise ties a
thread on your journal.

**You carry three things in:** the journal, the scarf (your vale stripes
came along; nobody comments; an ant-sized scarf is apparently fine), and
time.

## 2. The quiet throughline: Rove

Every page has its mapmaker. The House's is **Rove** — the colony's
surveyor, who mapped every room with a sprinkle jar and a straight face.
Her method survives her absence: **she marked her routes with sugar
sprinkles**, one color per route, pressed into dust and carpet weave and
the gaps between floorboards. The sprinkle lines are the House's pear
trees: follow a color long enough and you're somewhere she meant you to
be.

A year ago (nobody keeps years; call it "before the new sofa") she went
up the hatch to map the Attic, and she has been up there since. Not lost.
*Working.* On warm days the heating vent breathes her pages down into
Skirting Town — field notes in a fast hand, sometimes with a sprinkle
taped to the corner meaning *route continues*.

Nobody says "find Rove." The House just leans upward — the stairs, the
bookshelf, the hatch — and every sprinkle line, followed far enough,
bends toward the attic.

**The last page:** the Attic, when you finally climb to it, is the
House's held breath — white dust-sheet hills, boxes of the Humans'
younger lives, light through one small window. And in the middle, on a
sheet-draped table: **the Dollhouse.** A human house at ant scale. Door
ajar. Inside: rooms your size, a kitchen your size, a staircase built
for exactly you — the only place in the world that fits. And in the
dollhouse's littlest room stands a dollhouse of its own, perfectly
made, door ajar. What's inside THAT one is the House's kept mystery,
and the answer is NEVER (see the Ledger). Rove's newest map is spread
on the dollhouse table: the whole House, finished except one edge — and
on it, small, drawn in walking-ink: you, on the routes you actually
took. Her map was recording you. The world explored back.

Past the dollhouse's rear window, the attic wall has a knot-hole, and
through it: Outside, in pencil, inking itself green wherever you look.
A small figure on the garden wall straightens up and waves. Once.

## 3. The regions

Gestures per Law 2; every placed thing carries its why per Law 8;
◆ marks ledger rows. Color keys per [COLOR.md](COLOR.md). Density per
Amendment II: every screenful, three temptations.

### 3.1 Skirting Town — home

The colony city in the wall gap between kitchen and living room, lit by
a salvaged string of fairy lights (Main Street glows amber, pink, mint —
the bulbs that still work are named). *You arrive here your first
night, carried in half-asleep by the forage line that found you in the
strawberry bag.*

- **The matchbox hall.** Mayor Bristle's office: a matchbox on its side,
  a thimble on a plinth ("the Founding Thimble"), the town charter
  chalked on slate: RULES: 1. SHARE. 2. WAVE BACK. 3. NOT ON VACUUM DAY.
- **Main Street.** Bottle-cap tables, stamp posters (the Humans' stamps:
  ants believe they are portraits of ancestors ◆ — answered: they're
  stamps; one ant knows; nobody believes him), the Share (the common
  crumb store, a proud little granary of a bread-tag silo).
- **Pip's Museum of Important Artifacts.** A kid ant's shelf of human
  objects, every label wrong with total confidence: a guitar pick
  ("DRAGON SCALE"), a SIM card ("THE TINY DOOR"), a die ("CALENDAR"),
  a AAA battery ("THE HEAVY"). Bring him finds; watch him label them;
  correcting him is forbidden by him.
- **The vent.** Where Rove's pages arrive on warm days. There's a bench
  under it. There's always somebody on the bench.
- **The notice slate:** "PETALS? NO — CRUMB FORECAST: GOOD. TOAST AT
  7:40 AS USUAL. VACUUM: THEY WATERED THE PLANTS. YOU KNOW WHAT THAT
  MEANS."

*Gestures:* fairy-light glow through wall cracks; the smell of the Share;
the town hums faintly with the fridge's note next door.

### 3.2 The Kitchen — the breadbasket

Counter mesas, tile flats, the great appliances. The richest and busiest
country, run to Marm's forage schedule.

- **The counter mesas.** Reached by the drawer-handle ladder line (a
  known climb, polished by feet). Up top: the Crumb Runs (toast fallout
  zone, best at 7:41), the fruit bowl (the Orchard — a banana is a
  landscape event with a ripeness forecast), the knife block (respected,
  skirted, never discussed).
- **The Coldworks (the fridge).** The humming glacier. Its note never
  stops ◆ (the colony's song is tuned to it — "B-flat! I checked with my
  legs!"). When a Human opens it: **the Opening** — cold fog rolls off
  the shelf-cliffs and the light pours out gold-white: the House's
  aurora, twice or thrice a day, schedule learnable. Riding IN is
  forbidden by Rule 3-adjacent common sense; standing in the fog is
  everyone's favorite free wonder.
- **The sugar mine.** The bowl. Glittering scree. The Share's richest
  seam. Worked politely: never from the top where They'd notice.
- **The tap.** The waterfall. Drips on a beat you can set a stroll by;
  the basin is the harbor; a teaspoon left in it is the pier.
- **The toaster.** The volcano. Erupts at 7:40 sharp on weekdays ◆ (on
  Sundays it doesn't — answered: pancakes). Eruption showers the west
  counter with crumb. Marm's crews are in position by 7:38.
- **The under-fridge.** The dark wilds. Dust bunnies live here — soft
  grey fauna, drifting in herds, harmless, faintly majestic ◆ (do they
  eat? — never observed eating; the ledger holds the question). A lost
  fridge magnet letter "R" leans in the dark like a monument.
- **The sill garden.** Herb pots on the kitchen sill: the basil forest,
  the rosemary ridge — an indoor Outside, visited daily by **the Bee**
  through the cracked window: enormous, gentle, the House's whale. She
  says one word. It is always the same word.
- **The compost caddy.** The bog. Rich, dramatic, socially complicated.
  A place people say they never go, while visibly chewing.

*Gestures:* toast smell at 7:40; the fridge fog; sprinkle line ORANGE
runs the plinth from town to the sugar mine.

### 3.3 The Living Room — the savanna

Carpet you wade through, furniture ranges, the Screen, and the House's
migrating rainbow.

- **The carpet savanna.** Pile so deep it's tallgrass. Crumbs here are
  wild game, not farm yield. A lost earring stands mid-carpet like a
  standing stone; nobody moved it; it has a name (the Glint).
- **The Couch Mountains.** Cushion passes, armrest summits. And beneath:
  **the Under-Couch** — the lost stratum: coins, a remote control
  (◆ "the Wand They mourn" — They still look for it; the ants know
  exactly where it is; a whole ethics debate at town meetings), crisps
  of great age, one (1) sock. Archaeology by lamplight. Digs leave the
  journal full of rubbings.
- **The Screen (the TV).** At night the Humans light it and the whole
  room goes blue: **the Blue Hour.** Ants gather on the bookshelf ledge
  and watch — the colony's cinema, plots reported wrongly the next day
  with total confidence. When it's off, its black face reflects the
  room: the other place ants wave at (see the Backwards Town, §3.5).
- **The Bright.** The window has a hanging crystal (a sun-catcher). On
  clear mornings it throws a patch of rainbow on the floor — and the
  patch MOVES, creeping wall to wall with the sun. The ants treat it as
  a creature: it migrates, it has moods, you can stand in it (everyone
  stands in it; standing in the Bright is the House's Rose window).
  ◆ One ant knows it's the crystal. "I've SEEN the crystal!" Nobody
  believes her. (Answered, disbelieved — the ledger's favorite kind.)
- **The radiator.** The hot springs. Winter resort. The Dog's spot is
  adjacent; see the Dog.
- **The rug fringe.** The reed bed. Kids dare each other into it.
  There is nothing in it. There is definitely nothing in it. ◆
- **The bookshelf face.** A climbing country in itself: spine ledges,
  the dictionary overhang, the framed photo terrace (the Humans,
  younger, at a beach — the ants' one window into Their mythology).

*Gestures:* the Bright's daily crossing; TV glow under the door at
night; sprinkle line GREEN runs the skirting to the bookshelf base.

### 3.4 The Hallway — the pass

The tide-gate country: draughts, arrivals, departures, the mail.

- **The Big Door.** Opens twice a day (the Leaving ~8:15, the Returning
  ~18:05) with a wind you can lean on. For six seconds the House touches
  Outside: smells pour in (cut grass! rain! bins!) and every ant in
  earshot stops to read the air.
- **The mail slot.** At ~13:00 the slot bangs and **the Meteor** falls —
  paper the size of a tennis court, drifting down the hall. Mail-day
  salvage is a sport: rubber bands (bridges), stamps (portraits?!),
  window envelopes (glass for Skirting Town). A postcard is a holiday:
  a picture of Somewhere Else, publicly read, wrongly interpreted.
- **The key bowl.** The bell of the House: when keys land in it, that's
  the Returning made audible. Ants set their evening by the key-clatter.
- **The coat cliffs.** Pockets are caves with weather (mint dust,
  receipt drifts, once a whole biscuit — the Biscuit Winter, still
  toasted at festivals).
- **The Roomba dock.** The ferry berth. **The Roomba** (town name: Big
  Smooth) runs its rounds on schedule, and riding it is the House's
  ferry: hop the bumper at the dock, ride the great slow arcs of the
  downstairs floor, hop off anywhere. Its light changes color by mood
  (docked: green; roaming: white; stuck under the couch: red, and it
  sings its little distress note until Someone comes — riding out a
  couch-rescue is the connoisseur's trip).
- **The stairs.** Switchback country to Upstairs; each riser a cliff
  with a known route. **The banister is the fast way down** — the
  House's great slide, waxed by generations. **Envelope sledding** on
  the stair carpet is the post-day sport.

*Gestures:* draught under the Big Door; the Meteor's bang; sprinkle line
BLUE goes upstairs, riser by riser.

### 3.5 Upstairs — the Study and the Bathroom

**The Study — the archive.**
- **Bookshelf city.** Spines as streets. **Sylvia** the silverfish
  librarian lives in the dictionary and eats one word per book — only
  the boring ones, she insists ("'Whereas.' 'Notwithstanding.'
  Delicious."). Borrowing works on trust; returning works on asks.
- **The desk.** The keyboard plains (each key a tile with a letter —
  ants hold spelling festivals on it at night ◆ — what did they spell
  that one morning the Humans found "hi" typed? — reserved row), the
  pen pot (the timber yard), the sticky-note stack (the colony's paper
  quarry — Skirting Town's notices are all yellow), the mug of cold tea
  (the lake district; NOT for swimming; someone always swims).
- **The lamp.** The warm sun of the study, switched by weather (Them).
  **Vesper the moth** orbits it nightly and is in a complicated
  relationship with it. Walking Vesper home to the lampshade is a
  nightly ask that everyone takes turns pretending is a chore.
- **The Blinkbox (the router).** On the top shelf, blinking. ◆ Always
  the same rhythm, and one ant keeps vigil with tally-sheets, convinced
  it is saying something. It blinks. It has always blinked. (NEVER.)
- **The printer.** The oracle. Rarely, unprompted at 2am, it hums, and
  the whole shelf holds its breath. Once it printed a page of nothing.
  The page is in Pip's museum ("THE PROPHECY").

**The Bathroom — the rainlands.**
- **The steam jungle.** Twice a day the shower runs and the room goes
  rainforest: fog, dripping cliffs of tile, the mirror opaque. Ferns of
  steam curl off the towel rail. Everything glitters for an hour after.
- **The Backwards Town.** The mirror over the sink. In it: a colony
  exactly like yours, waving exactly when you wave. ◆ The ledger does
  not rule on the Backwards Town. Go press your hand to theirs and
  decide for yourself. (The chorus is split. The debate is ancient. It
  is the town's favorite argument and nobody wants it settled.)
- **The toothpaste cap glacier.** Mint snow. A delicacy? A hazard? Both.
- **The scale.** The trampoline. It shows numbers when you bounce.
  Nobody knows what the numbers mean. The record is 0.2.
- **The Deep.** The drain. Folklore says a town called Drainton, drowned
  and golden, lies below ◆. Folklore says a lot of things. The grate
  breathes cool air, and every kid has dared the third rung down, and
  the third rung is the agreed edge of the world.

### 3.6 The Attic — the last page

Reached by the hatch (Dog-assisted, see the Dog) or the long wall-climb
inside the airing cupboard. Dust-sheet hills, box canyons of Their
younger lives (a trophy! a tiny guitar! letters tied in ribbon — read
one aloud at the vent and the whole town goes quiet), the single window,
the smell of warm wood. And the Dollhouse (§2). The House's held
breath. Guidance budget here: nearly zero. By the time you climb the
hatch, the whole House has been one long gesture pointing at it.

## 4. The Household (the weather)

The Humans are never villains and never fully seen — knees down, hands,
voices through floors (their speech renders as muffled music, never
words ◆ — except once; reserved row). They are the world's weather and
its geology, and their schedule is the clock:

| hour | event | what it means at ant height |
|---|---|---|
| 7:00 | the Quake | the alarm; the bed thunders; the House wakes |
| 7:40 | the Eruption | toast; crumb weather on the west counter |
| 8:15 | the Leaving | the Big Door tide; the House opens; ants' true day begins |
| ~13:00 | the Meteor | mail; salvage sport |
| 17:50 | the Dog stirs | ten-minute warning, reliable as tides |
| 18:05 | the Returning | keys in the bowl; cozy curfew; kitchen weather all evening |
| ~21:00 | the Blue Hour | the Screen lights; cinema on the bookshelf ledge |
| 23:00 | the Big Dark | lamps off; the LED constellation comes out (see COLOR.md) |

**Weekly:** Vacuum Day — the Storm. Announced a day early by the plants
being watered (the ants read this omen with total confidence and they
are always right). The colony shutters, the wilds are scoured clean,
and the crumb economy resets ("a hard reset with a lesson in it" —
Marm). Nobody can be caught: shelter is everywhere, the walls are
always open, and the one ant who ever rode the bag got shaken out with
the bin dust and walked home famous. **Sunday:** no Eruption; pancake
smell; the long lie-in makes the whole House late and golden.
**Rarely:** Guests (crumb bloom, coats to explore, a handbag like a
travelling country); bubble-bath night (the bathroom goes full prism);
the Argument About The Broken Lamp (they keep it ◆ — answered: each
thinks the other loves it).

**The Dog.** Weather with a face, and the House's secret ally. The Dog
has always known about the colony ◆ — watch long enough and it looks
straight at you and drops a crumb. On purpose. (Answered, gloriously.)
The Dog's spot is by the radiator; the Dog's tail is a bridge that
wags; late in the game, the Dog will lie down at the stairs' base and
let you ride to the top — and, once, all the way to below the attic
hatch, and nose it open, and pretend to be asleep.

## 5. The people

Nine majors plus the chorus. Voices, grammars, and the full sample banks
live in [VOICES.md](VOICES.md) — everything text-box shaped.

| major | where | the bit |
|---|---|---|
| **Mayor Bristle** | matchbox hall | confirms rumors "as mayor"; ceremonial about everything |
| **Marm** | the forage lines | crumb reports like weather reports; spends no words |
| **Pip** | his museum | labels every artifact wrong; corrections forbidden |
| **Sylvia** | the dictionary | eats only the boring words; overdue books grieve her |
| **Vera** | under the stairs | a spider who thinks she's terrifying; knits with sock wool |
| **Vesper** | the study lamp | a moth in a complicated relationship with the Light |
| **Rollo** | anywhere, mid-roll | a woodlouse; speaks only while rolling; doppler delivery |
| **The Bee** | the sill garden | one word. Always the same word. |
| **Rove** | the attic | pages only, down the vent; sprinkle-marked routes |

**Presences:** the Dog (one wink, one crumb, one ride), the Humans
(weather), the Backwards Town (waves when you do), Big Smooth (the
Roomba; green when docked; sings when stuck).

**The chorus** is big and modern: the B-flat checker, the skirting
enthusiast ("Straight for THREE WHOLE ROOMS!"), the Roomba conductor
("Next stop: UNDER THE COUCH. Mind the sock."), the fridge-aurora
regular ("Opening at six! Yogurt's going in! You don't want to miss the
light!"), the sock philosopher, Aunt Tessie's nephew ("She napped on
the warm rectangle. Got texted to Denmark. She's FINE. She came back
by post."), the toast sommelier ("Sourdough again. Bold house."), the
stair-sled kids, the two elders who argue about the Backwards Town
professionally. One great line each. Density is people.

## 6. The doings

- **Ride Big Smooth** — the Roomba ferry: hop on at the dock, ride the
  slow arcs, hop off anywhere. Couch-rescue rides for connoisseurs.
- **Climb everything** — walls, curtains, table legs; hang from the
  lampshade over the living room and the camera breathes out: the whole
  room below, tiny and alive. The House's Overlook is a light fixture.
- **Stand in the Bright** — the migrating rainbow patch; your scarf
  takes the colors; the journal sketches you prismatic.
- **Stand in the Opening** — fridge fog rolling over you, gold light,
  everyone's free aurora.
- **Envelope-sled the stairs** — post-day sport; the landing counts.
- **Ride the banister** — the fast way down; generations of wax.
- **Surf the Screen static** — walk the TV face right after it's
  switched off; your whole body's fur stands up; kids scream happily.
- **Dig the Under-Couch** — archaeology; rubbings into the journal;
  ethics debates about the Wand.
- **Bounce the scale** — the record is 0.2. The record can be beaten.
- **Spell on the keyboard** — at night, with friends, one tile each.
- **Read the air at the Big Door** — six seconds of Outside, twice a
  day; the journal keeps a smell diary in scribbled colors.
- **Sled the toothpaste cap, swim the tea (forbidden, constant), walk
  Vesper home, wave at the Backwards Town, press flowers from the sill
  garden, carry one thing in your hands** — your hands are still the
  inventory.

## 7. The asks

Same law as everywhere (Amendment I): face to face, refusable, strings,
no log, worldly rewards.

- **The 7:38 Run.** Marm: "Toast at 7:40. West counter. Carry what you
  can." Your first shift on a forage line — the House's Bread Round.
- **The Museum Donation.** Pip wants "a dragon scale but BIGGER." What
  you bring, he labels. Wrong. Forever. The label is the reward.
- **Sylvia's Overdue.** A book went downstairs "before the new sofa."
  Find it (it's propping the wobbly table — a known scandal), get it up
  three books of shelf. She re-shelves it and eats one "notwithstanding"
  in celebration.
- **Wool for Vera.** She's out of sock. Wash Day is tomorrow. The dryer
  lint trap is right there. Nobody said heist. (It's a heist.)
- **Walk Vesper Home.** Nightly. "Just to the lampshade." He will talk
  about the Light the entire way. It is somehow never annoying twice
  the same way.
- **The Vigil Relief.** The Blinkbox watcher needs one night off in
  forever. Sit the vigil. Tally the blinks. See absolutely nothing.
  Understand completely why she watches.
- **Founding Day Polish.** Mayor Bristle, ceremonially: the Thimble
  must shine. The polishing cloth is bigger than four ants. It becomes
  a parade float. This was maybe the plan.
- **The Couch Rescue.** Big Smooth is stuck and singing. Ride-along
  with the freeing crew; be the one who pushes the sock clear; get
  saluted by the conductor every ride thereafter.
- **Where's Pip.** Recurring: he's "on expedition" (stowed away on Big
  Smooth again). He is never lost. His mother knows. You have legs.

## 8. The tune

The House's melody assembles like the vale's did, from its own stuff:
the fridge's endless root note; the microwave's ding (a quarter-tone
sharp, beloved); the wind-chime outside the cracked window; the Quake
alarm's first three notes; and — ◆ — a half-phrase one of the Humans
whistles doing dishes, which the ants already know, and nobody can say
where They learned it. Beyond the dollhouse's knot-hole window, it
plays whole.

## 9. Scope, honestly

7 regions + the attic; ~55 authored places; 9 majors + ~20 chorus + 4
presences; the Household schedule as clock; one melody; one Bright.
Ledger opens at 18 rows: 13 answered, 4 NEVER, 1 reserved. First
playable spine (bag-in → Skirting Town → the 7:38 Run → first Bright)
is the proof-of-house, gated exactly like the vale's.

## 10. The title that learns

The page opens on **somewhere warm**. When a local first says the words
— "welcome to the Great Indoors; mind the gap in Rule 3" — the title
learns: **THE GREAT INDOORS**, that copy, forever.
