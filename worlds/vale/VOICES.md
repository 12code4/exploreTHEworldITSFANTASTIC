# VOICES — WENNOW VALE

Ruled by the root [DIALOGUE.md](../../DIALOGUE.md) (the Text Box Law).
Every line below is text-box shaped: one or two short sentences, one bit
per person, no stage directions doing the work.

## The majors

### ODD BOLE — the bit: rates everything, spends no words
Grammar: seven words or fewer. Weather first. Drops pronouns.
- "Wind's gone round. Sit middle."
- "Seven. Tide helped."
- "Four. Don't blame the wind."
- "Herons had the rock by six."
- "Noon run. Was noon when I said it."
- *(follow-up)* "Still noon."
- *(after the fog watch)* "Right then, ___." *(your name. He's already walking.)*

### PERL — the bit: never asks for help, describes situations until they're yours
Grammar: talks to the dough and the oven as colleagues. Counts things.
- "Eight loaves behind. Don't help. Stand somewhere useful."
- "Loaf's for the Locks. Gets stale or gets carried."
- *(follow-up)* "Cloth it or it rides wet."
- "Tell her the crust's on purpose this time!"
- "That's the third gull today with opinions."
- "Sill loaf's the custom. Take it. No ceremony!"
- *(follow-up)* "The last one who made a ceremony wept."

### IRIS KELL — the bit: understatement with a delay on it
Grammar: two beats. The second arrives late or never. Calls the lighthouse "the work."
- "Sevens. Suits the water."
- "Mother kept sevens. Somebody started it."
- *(follow-up)* "Nobody finished it."
- "Door's ajar means come up."
- *(follow-up)* "Shut means come up quieter."
- *(the once)* "Slow. It was never a race."

### FEN — the bit: numbers out loud, one swerve per conversation
Grammar: precise, then suddenly not. Tea ends feelings.
- "Six thousand and forty-one nights. All sevens."
- *(follow-up)* "Tea?"
- "Stand here. No — here. Now wait."
- *(follow-up)* "There! Nobody builds that. It just happens."
- "Once I dreamt it did eight. Never told the lighthouse."
- "To the keeper. Direct. Go before I—" *(he will catch you at her door)*
- "Changed the wording! One word! It's a comma, at root."

### ADA — the bit: the vale's whole aphorism budget, half spent undercutting it
Grammar: stories with detours. Cats as punctuation.
- "Nim never forgave us for the bell. Cats keep accounts."
- "A question you can keep is worth two you've spent."
- *(follow-up)* "My mother said that. She also ate a wasp once."
- "Tansy! Off the butter!"
- "Grim's not lost. He's somewhere warm being magnificent."
- *(follow-up)* "And I'm old and you have legs."
- "You carry the lantern. I got good at the watching."

### SELA — the bit: second halves of thoughts only
Grammar: the first half happened privately. Homeric on one subject: the winch.
- "...which is why the gates stick Thursdays."
- "So she's still on about the crust."
- *(follow-up)* "Hold the middle rope."
- "Page came Tuesday. Alder's down. Busy week upstream."
- "Read it out. Slower. She writes at walking pace."
- "Alder. Like all-and-her. No d in this whole valley."
- *(the winch, if started)* "The GOOD winch had a handle filed to fit a WORKING hand—" *(sit down; this runs long; it's better than the radio)*

### LITTLE WICK — the bit: rules invented mid-sentence, binding immediately
Grammar: kid-certainty. Owns the banned words, uses them wrong.
- "It's not lost! The sea is HOLDING it. That's different."
- "You owe me a green one now. It's the rules. I just made them."
- "Everyone says there's no pond. Everyone hasn't been!"
- "The Looker makes everything go church windows. You can't have it."
- *(follow-up)* "...One marble. Tell the sea it owes me."
- "Look at the bell with it first! The bell goes mysterious!"
- "I drew Odd's boat with legs and he looked at it for AGES."

### NETTLE — the bit: colors as verbs, moods, and weather
Grammar: talks to the pools like livestock. Barters adjectives.
- "Sky's doing a weld-yellow. Won't last."
- *(follow-up)* "Nothing yellow lasts."
- "Settle! You're not the sea. Nobody said you were the sea."
- "She's on umber now. Umber means rock. Rock means up."
- "One pool. Pick with your chest, not your eyes."
- *(after your dip)* "Madder. Bold for a Tuesday."
- *(the stripe)* "Wasn't me. Pool did that on its own."
- *(rain rush, after)* "You're rained on. Good. Means it counted."

### HOB — the bit: none. A wave.
No lines. Once, late, the fire left lit for you is the sentence.

### MAREN — pages only
Grammar: verbs first, no "I." The heart sneaks in, in parentheses.
- "Alder down. Kingfisher approves. Moved the bench accordingly."
- "Echo answers twice. Counted. Recounted. Kept."
- "Out of green. Umber now. (Thank her properly for the green.)"
- "Somebody's walking the vale small and slow, the right way. (Plant a pear for them.)"

## The chorus

One line each, two at most. They make the vale crowded with life without
growing the knowable cast. Market mornings and Lantern Nights, the chorus
doubles.

- **The post:** "The knee knows rain. The knee is never wrong."
- **The curate:** "I wept at the loaf once. We don't discuss it."
- **Market granny:** "Sixty years at this stall. The pears fear me."
- **Kid on the stepping stones:** "Step wrong and the river COUNTS it!"
- **Second kid:** "It's true! It counted my brother!"
- **Man outside the chapel:** "I only go for the window. Don't tell the curate."
- **Woman with a basket:** "Petals went up again last night. UP."
- **Old fisherman:** "The sea's fine. It's the land I watch."
- **Fen's rival (self-declared):** "I count the STARS. Anyone can count a lighthouse."
- **Ferry regular:** "The cat's fare is a sardine. Mine's a nod. Fair's fair."
- **Granny at her window:** "That's Maren's tree, that one. And that one. And that one."
- **Boy with a jar:** "I'm collecting fog. Nearly got some."
