# THE MYSTERY LEDGER

Every question Wennow Vale raises, and where its answer lives — or the
word **NEVER**. The ledger keeps us honest in both directions: no
accidental tollbooths (every answered mystery's answer must actually be
reachable in-world, by noticing, never by grinding), and no accidentally
exhausted world (the NEVER rows are permanent; do not "improve" them with
answers — they are load-bearing).

Rules of the ledger:

1. A mystery is **noticed, never assigned**. Nothing here may ever appear
   as an objective, hint popup, or journal to-do. The journal may sketch
   what the player has seen; it never asks a question for them.
2. Answers resolve into **story or charm, never loot**.
3. New mysteries added during the build get a row before they ship.
4. The tithe (roughly one in ten of ALL the vale's questions, small ones
   included) stays NEVER. The famous mysteries run richer than a tenth on
   purpose — fame should attach to the unsolvable. Audit at milestone
   *The Tithe*.

| # | The question | Where the answer lives |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Why does the lighthouse blink in sevens? | **NEVER.** Folklore only: Iris ("my mother kept sevens"), Fen's tallies, three villagers with three contradictory sweet stories. |
| 2 | Seven chimneys, six hearths — whose is the seventh? Why is it warm at dusk? What is behind the little soot-door with no keyhole? | **NEVER.** The vale's unopenable door. It never opens. |
| 3 | Why does the echo at the Steps answer twice? | **NEVER.** Maren's way-mark states the fact; the fact is true; that is all. |
| 4 | What is the Green Evening? | **NEVER.** It happens, rarely. Fen tallies those too. |
| 5 | Why do petals float UPSTREAM at dusk? | Sela's laundry points up-valley every evening ("the sea breathes in of an evening") + the hanging orchard above the gorge supplies the blossom. Answer assembled by noticing wind + trees; taught by a clothesline. |
| 6 | `M + T` carved on the third stepping stone — who? | The Nine Cats: the stone carved TANSY, plus Ada's stories. Maren and her cat. A joke carved by a girl, weathered into a romance by guessers. |
| 7 | Nine stones, and Ada names ten cats — where is the tenth stone? | The chapel doorstep at the Cross: socket-shaped hollow on the moor, oddly-shaped doorstep, worn carving `OLD NIM`, Ada's "moved for luck," and the bell that cracked the year it was lifted. Four details re-lit by one noticing. Model mystery. |
| 8 | Little Wick's chalk map shows a pond no adult remembers — is it real? | The Chalk Pond is in the Hushes, exactly where she chalks it. Children go everywhere. |
| 9 | Who burns charcoal in the Hushes by night? | Hob, Perl's shy brother — only ever seen leaving. Perl says "my brother," fondly, if pears are involved. |
| 10 | Why is there sometimes a marble in the tide pools? | Little Wick loses them off the jetty ("trades with the mermaids, obviously"); the tide re-deals them. |
| 11 | The boathouse hull is patched with map-paper — whose map? | Maren's spoiled first survey sheet; Iris: "she papered half the vale." |
| 12 | How do Maren's pages get into the eel baskets? | She drops them upstream in oilcloth; the traps catch what the river carries. Sela says exactly this, shrugging. |
| 13 | Who is up past the Steps, and why do the pear trees make a line? | Maren, drawing the next page. The trees are her survey stations — she paid herself in pear trees. The line is her route. |
| 14 | The milestone reads `ACROSS —` with the distance chiselled out — who, and why? | **NEVER** decided lightly — keep it small: no one alive says. (Counts toward the tithe as a minor never; revisit at audit only to confirm it stayed minor.) |
| 15 | Why does the bell ring its third note flat? | The crack — the year Old Nim's stone was lifted (see #7). The tune carries the flat third everywhere the bell is heard. |
| 16 | Whose gloves are on the notice board? | Sela's — she never claims them because "they dry better up there." Locals know; asking is the answer. |
| 17 | Why does the chapel cat attend services? | The chapel's morning sunbeam lands on the front pew. Observable any clear morning. |
| 18 | Who wins the Good Rock, herons or gulls? | Sit and watch enough mornings: herons at dawn, gulls by midmorning, herons again when the ferry wake settles. The rock has a schedule like everyone else. |
| 19 | Do Maren's way-marks tell the truth? | Yes — every one is testable and true (kestrel in May, the doubled echo, all of them). The world honors its own details; this is the vale teaching the player to trust it. |

Tally at opening: 19 questions · 15 answered in-world · 4 NEVER
(#1, #2, #3, #4) with #14 held as a minor never — the famous four are
famous BECAUSE they are the tithe.
