# THE VOICE BIBLE

How people talk in Wennow Vale — and how they must never talk.

## The disease, named

There is a way AI-flavored game dialogue goes wrong, and this project is
susceptible, so the failure is named here and checked at every review:

- **Everyone is wise.** Every line lands a little button of meaning.
  Real people mostly land nothing; the meaning leaks out sideways.
- **Everyone is the same person** — warm, wry, faintly wistful, fond of
  em-dashes. A cast of one voice in nine hats.
- **People answer the question.** Real people answer some other question,
  the one they were already thinking about.
- **The theme gets said out loud.** The moment anyone in the vale says
  something true about wonder or exploration, the line is dead.
- **Lines are complete.** Real speech is interrupted — by weather, dough,
  cats, and the speaker's own next thought.

The cure is mechanical, not aspirational: **every voice is a grammar** —
a set of syntax rules a line can be checked against — and every line must
pass the tired-person test: *would this human say this, at this hour, with
their hands full?*

## The rules

1. **Nobody says the theme.** Banned in dialogue: *fantastic, wonder,
   mystery, explore, journey, magical.* (Exception: Wick, misusing them.)
2. **One aphorism budget for the whole vale.** Only Ada lands wisdom, at
   most once an evening, and she undercuts half of it herself.
3. **Talk about things, not feelings.** Weather, knees, gulls, dough,
   rope, tide, socks. Feelings arrive by carrier pigeon, disguised as
   things.
4. **Lines are work-shaped.** People talk while doing; the doing
   interrupts; the doing wins. Punctuate with the task, not with poetry.
5. **Nobody explains the vale.** History arrives in complaint form,
   sideways, unfinished. (You can get Sela started on the winch. That IS
   the lore delivery system.)
6. **No line repeats within a day**, lines rotate by clock, weather, and
   what has actually happened — and **news walks** (a thing you did
   travels person-to-person at their real meetings, arriving slightly
   wrong).
7. **The traveler is silent.** The player speaks in gestures — nod,
   shake, shrug, point, show-hands, small bow, show-the-flyleaf.
   Villagers over-read these generously. That gap is where the comedy
   and the tenderness both live.
8. **Read every line aloud.** If it sounds like embroidery, cut it. If
   any other character could say it, cut it. If it would survive on a
   greeting card, burn it.

## The voices

Each entry: the grammar (enforceable), then sample lines — written as
they'd be heard, at their hour, mid-task.

### ODD BOLE — the ferryman
**Grammar:** seven words or fewer, usually. Weather first, then the
point, if any. Drops pronouns like they're heavy. One question per
season. Never says goodbye; the boat says it.

- "Wind's gone round. Sit middle."
- "Herons had the rock by six. Gulls filed a complaint." *(that's him
  hilarious; it took years)*
- "Noon run. Was noon when I said it."
- *(rating a stone skim)* "Seven. Tide helped."
- *(rating a worse one)* "Four. Don't feed it to me about the wind."
- *(offered a pear)* — takes it, holds it up like a toast, says nothing.
- *(fog)* "Bell. Sevens if it's you. Slow. I'll knock the answer."
- *(after the fog watch — the whole speech)* "Right then, ___." *(your
  name. First time. He's already walking away.)*

### PERL — the baker
**Grammar:** run-on lines kneaded in a working rhythm, interrupted by the
dough and the oven, both of which she addresses as colleagues. Counts
everything. Never asks for help; describes a situation until it's yours.

- "Third gull today with opinions, that one — no you don't — sorry,
  the crust. You were saying. You weren't. Say something anyway, I'm
  eight loaves behind."
- "Sill loaf's the custom, take it, don't make a ceremony, the last one
  who made a ceremony was a curate and he wept and it held up the queue."
- "Loaf's hers. Walks up Tuesdays with the post but the post's knee is
  doing its thing. I'm not asking anything. Loaf just sits there. Gets
  stale or gets carried, no business of mine which."
- *(you take it)* "Wind it in the cloth or it rides wet. Tell her the
  crust's on purpose this time."
- *(re: Hob)* "My brother. Out clarting about with trees at all hours.
  He'll wave. That's a whole conversation, from him, you got the
  long version."
- *(dawn, to the oven)* "Don't sulk. It's the same wood as yesterday."

### IRIS KELL — the keeper
**Grammar:** understatement as a load-bearing wall. Calls the lighthouse
"the work." Two-beat replies; the second beat arrives later than
expected, or not at all. Weathers-in her feelings so thoroughly they read
as facts about brass.

- "Sevens." *(polishes)* "Suits the water."
- "My mother kept sevens. Her mother kept sevens. Somebody started it."
  *(long pause, lamp cloth)* "Nobody finished it."
- "Door's ajar means come up. Door's shut means come up quieter."
- *(re: Fen, unprompted, apropos of polish)* "That one on the ridge has
  been not-sending me a letter for eleven years. Best correspondence I
  ever had."
- *(letting you light the lamp, once, after everything)* "Slow. It's not
  a race." *(beat)* "It was never a race."

### FEN — the watcher on the ridge
**Grammar:** precision spirals — numbers read aloud like they're
beautiful, because to him they are — then one swerve per conversation
into the thing he actually means. Tea is deployed to end feelings.

- "Six thousand and forty-one. Forty-two tonight, weather permitting.
  It won't. Tea?"
- "You'll want to stand here. No — here. The lamp, the water, the gap.
  Now wait." *(you wait)* "There. Nobody builds that. It just happens
  every day."
- *(the dream, eventually, to the kettle rather than to you)* "Once — I'd
  been up forty hours, mind — I dreamt it did eight. Eight. I have never
  told the lighthouse this."
- *(handing you the letter)* "To the keeper. Direct. No — steady hands.
  Yours are steady? Look at them. Fine. Fine. Go before I."
- *(catching you at the lighthouse door, breathless)* "Changed the
  wording. One word. Give it — thank you — nothing personal, the wording
  was — it's a comma, really, at root." *(This has happened to everyone
  in the vale. Iris knows. Everyone knows.)*

### ADA — grandmother's cottage
**Grammar:** stories that take the long way and arrive somewhere better
than they aimed. Addresses cats mid-clause as punctuation. Owner of the
vale's entire aphorism budget, half of it spent undercutting itself.

- "— and that was the year the bell went flat, which Nim never forgave
  us, being under the chapel door by then — Tansy, off the butter — under
  the DOOR, I said, which is lucky, a door should have a cat in it
  somewhere, that's just building sense."
- "Moss was the fast one. Named half this village after cats, you know,
  or the cats after the village, my mother would never say which, she
  thought a question you could keep was worth two you'd spent."
  *(beat)* "She also ate a wasp once. On purpose. So."
- "Grim's off again. He isn't lost. He has never once been lost. But
  he's somewhere warm being magnificent and I'm old and you have legs."
- *(lantern night, handing it over)* "Knees used to do that bank in the
  dark carrying two of these and your grandmother — somebody's
  grandmother — singing. You carry it. I'll do the watching. Watching's
  the part I got good at."

### SELA — the lock keeper
**Grammar:** speaks in second halves of thoughts; the first halves
happened privately, earlier. Comfortable silence is a room she owns.
The winch is the exception: on the winch she is Homeric.

- "...which is why the gates stick Thursdays." *(no first half is
  coming)*
- *(taking the bread)* "So she's still on about the crust." *(beat)*
  "You'll want the gate in a minute. Hold the middle rope."
- "Got a page Tuesday. Alder's fallen. That alder was holding the far
  bank's opinion of itself together, so. Busy week upstream."
- *(handing you the page, eyes elsewhere)* "Read it out. Slower than
  that. She writes at walking pace."
- *(you mispronounce alder)* "Alder. Like all-and-her. There's no d
  worth hearing in this whole valley."
- *(the winch, started)* "The GOOD winch — no, sit down, you asked —
  the good winch had a handle my father filed to fit a hand exactly, not
  any hand, a WORKING hand, and the flood took it to the sea, where fish
  are using it, badly, for nothing —" *(continues. People start her on
  this at gatherings, on purpose. It's better than the radio.)*

### LITTLE WICK — the chalker
**Grammar:** kid-certainty. Rules invented mid-sentence and binding
immediately. Taxonomies. The only person allowed the banned words, all
of them wrong.

- "It's not lost. The sea is holding it. That's different. You owe me a
  green one now, it's the rules, I just made them."
- "That's the pond. Everyone says there's no pond. Everyone hasn't been.
  I'm everyone's map now."
- "This is the Looker. It makes everything into church windows. You
  can't have it. ...You can have it if the sea pays me back. ONE marble.
  Tell it."
- "I drew Odd's boat with legs. He said boats don't have legs. His boat.
  With legs. He looked at it for AGES."
- *(solemn, trading the marble for the Looker)* "Deal's a deal. Look at
  the bell with it first. The bell goes mysterious." *(she means
  kaleidoscopic; she will not be corrected)*

### NETTLE — the dyer
**Grammar:** colors are verbs, moods, and weather. Sentences dyed
mid-way — they come out a different color than they went in. Barters in
adjectives. Talks to the pools like livestock.

- "Sky's doing a weld-yellow. Won't last. Nothing yellow lasts."
- *(to the woad vat, walking her morning round)* "Settle. You're not
  the sea. Nobody said you were the sea."
- "She's on umber now. Was years of green. You can tell where she's got
  to by what she asks for. Umber means rock. Rock means up. Up means
  I should plant more madder, she'll want reds for the finish."
- *(your first scarf dip)* "One pool. Pick with your chest, not your
  eyes." *(you dip)* "Hm. Madder. Bold for a Tuesday."
- *(the impossible stripe)* "Wasn't me. Pool did that on its own.
  ...Keep wearing it where the pool can see."
- *(rain rush, after, everyone soaked)* "You're rained on. Good. Means
  it counted."

### HOB — the burner
**Grammar:** none. He is a shape at the far edge of a clearing, already
leaving. He waves once. (Late in the game, exactly once, if you have
been kind to Perl and found the ring cold and sat by it anyway: he
leaves the fire lit for you and the fire is the sentence.)

### MAREN — pages only
**Grammar:** field notes. Verbs first. No "I." Weather, then work, then
— rarely, buried — the heart, in parentheses, as if it snuck in.

- "Alder down. Kingfisher approves. Moved the bench accordingly."
- "Rain four days. Drew the rain. It came out as the river's ghost,
  which is fair."
- "Out of green. Tell Nettle: umber now, and thank her for the years of
  green. (Tell her properly.)"
- "Echo here answers twice. Counted. Recounted. Kept."
- "Somebody's walking the vale small and slow, the right way. (Plant a
  pear for them if this reaches the yards.)"

## Two scenes, as proof of method

### Scene: the bread ask (morning, bakery)

*(You've been up-valley at least once. Perl is kneading. She does not
stop kneading at any point in this scene.)*

**PERL:** Down again, are you. No no, stay put, you're not in the way,
you're — actually shift a foot. There. *(beat, dough)* You've been up the
Locks way. Seen the state of her socks on that line. *(turns a loaf)*
This one's hers. Walks up Tuesdays with the post, but the post's knee is
doing its thing again. *(not looking up)* I'm not asking anything. Loaf
just sits there. Gets stale or gets carried, no business of mine which.

*(You pick it up. A string ties itself to your journal, with a little
sketch of flour.)*

**PERL:** Wind it in the cloth or it rides wet. Tell her the crust's on
purpose this time.

*(At the Locks, Sela takes it without turning around.)*

**SELA:** So she's still on about the crust. *(beat)* You'll want the
gate in a minute. Hold the middle rope.

*(No thank-you exists anywhere in this scene. The gate-ride up IS the
thank-you. The string unties itself somewhere over the second weir.)*

### Scene: the fog watch (a fog morning, the harbor bell)

**ODD:** *(materializing out of the white)* Bell. Sevens if it's you.
Slow. I'll knock the answer. *(gone)*

*(You ring. Seven, slow. You listen. Fog gives you nothing. You ring
again. Far off — or near, fog lies — knock-knock on wood, an oar against
a hull. You ring. Knock. You ring. The knocks get a shape to them. Then
the bow of the ferry finds the fog's edge like a whale surfacing, and
the ferry cat is sitting on the prow like this was all routine, which,
to be fair.)*

**ODD:** *(tying off, not looking at you)* Right then, ___.

*(Your name. He has never asked it. News walks. Somewhere back down the
line — the flyleaf, Perl, the pie at noon — it reached him, and he kept
it for this. That is the entire reward, and no line of dialogue anywhere
in this game may be spent explaining that it was one.)*
