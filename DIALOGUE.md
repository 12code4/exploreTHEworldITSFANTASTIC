# THE TEXT BOX LAW

Game-wide dialogue law for every world in the atlas. Per-world voices live
in `worlds/<name>/VOICES.md`; this file outranks them all.

## The disease, rediagnosed

The first voice bible cured "everyone is a wise poet" and accidentally
prescribed "everyone is a literary short story." Lines came with stage
directions, timed beats, trailing ellipses, subtext under management. That
is still writing for the page, and a game is not a page. It reads AIesque
because it is doing three jobs per line and visibly proud of all of them.

The best NPC dialogue ever written does ONE job per line and lands it in
the time it takes to walk past. Pokémon's shorts kid. Animal Crossing's
neighbor with one thing on their mind. Link's Awakening's weirdos.
Undertale's total commitment to the bit. That's the standard: **a text box
is a small window a moving player reads in two seconds.** Write for the
window.

## The law

1. **The text box is small.** One or two short sentences per box. A third
   sentence means a second box, and the second box must earn its button
   press.
2. **One bit per person.** Every character IS one thing — the crumb
   critic, the Light's ex, the curator who labels everything wrong — and
   their lines are that thing, said different ways. Major characters get
   a grammar on top of their bit; the bit comes first.
3. **Declare, don't perform.** No stage directions doing the emotional
   work, no "(beat)," no ellipsis-as-mood. The words alone, said flat,
   must land. (Ellipses exist; they are a budget item, not a house
   style.)
4. **Confident and wrong beats clever and right.** The funniest people in
   a game are certain. "This is a dragon scale. Don't say guitar."
5. **Weirdly specific beats poetic.** "It's straight for THREE WHOLE
   ROOMS!" outlives any sentence about wonder.
6. **Loops are honest.** Talk again: one follow-up box, then the loop.
   Nobody fakes infinite depth. Events swap lines — that's how the world
   shows it moved.
7. **Exclamation marks are free.** A text box is allowed to be loud.
   Wonder in a game sounds like "The fridge hums in B-flat! I checked
   with my legs!" — not like literature.
8. **The crowd is the lore.** World-facts arrive one line at a time from
   many mouths, never a paragraph from one. If a fact needs a paragraph,
   it becomes five villagers.
9. **The chorus is deep.** Beyond the major cast, every world keeps a
   chorus of minor folk — one great line each, two at most. Density of
   people is density of world. A room with three one-line strangers in it
   beats a room with none.
10. **Nobody says the theme.** Still. Forever. (Except the one child per
    world who misuses the banned words, wrongly, with total confidence.)

**The test:** read the line in two seconds while imagining you're mashing
A. If it lands, ship it. If it needs your voice actor face, cut it.

## What survives from v1

Voices are still grammars (per-character syntax constraints, lint-checked
like the `why` field). People still talk about things, not feelings. One
aphorism budget per world, held by its eldest, half spent undercutting
itself. History still arrives in complaint form. The traveler is still
silent and speaks in gestures.

What changed: the form. Every sample line in every VOICES.md is now
text-box shaped, and any line that isn't is a bug.
